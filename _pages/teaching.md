### Machine Learning for Safety-Critical Decision Making

**ELEC70122, Spring 2026/2027**

Machine learning is increasingly used to inform high-stakes decisions in healthcare, finance, criminal justice and defence. In these settings an accurate prediction is not enough: errors matter when they trigger actions, their consequences are rarely equal, and many failures only appear after deployment, when the data, population or environment has changed. Models that exploit spurious correlations, report overconfident probabilities or optimise the wrong objective can cause real harm.

Foundation models and large language models have made powerful general-purpose systems widely available, but pretraining alone does not ensure safe behaviour. Building models that can be trusted to act, or to support human decision makers, requires reasoning carefully about uncertainty, causality and evaluation.

In this course, you will learn how to build statistically sound ML models for risk-sensitive settings and how to judge when their outputs can be safely acted on. Topics include Bayesian modelling and posterior predictive decisions, calibration and conformal prediction, robustness to distribution shift, learning to defer to human experts, safe and offline reinforcement learning with constrained MDPs, off-policy evaluation and risk-sensitive value estimation, causal inference (causes of effects and effects of causes, treatment effect estimation), and alignment and auditing of foundation models through RLHF and inverse reinforcement learning. Methods are illustrated with real applications, including ICU decision making and HIV treatment optimisation.
